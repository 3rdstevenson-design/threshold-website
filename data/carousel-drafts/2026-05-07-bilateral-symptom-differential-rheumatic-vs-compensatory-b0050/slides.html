CLINICAL PATTERN

Both sides hurt. That changes the differential.

Most pain has one origin: how this person walks, sits, swings, sleeps. When the same picture shows up on both sides at the same time, load alone has a hard time explaining it.

02
THE TELL

Bilateral symptoms widen the picture.

One-sided pain usually traces to load history. When both sides match each other in tempo, location, and intensity, the cause is likely living above the muscle layer.

Both kneesBoth shouldersBoth hips

03
THE THREE BUCKETS

Three differentials live behind a bilateral picture.

Compensatory loading can mirror across symmetric athletes (lifters, runners, cyclists). Spinal-origin pain refers bilaterally when central or bilateral nerve roots are involved. Rheumatic disease is systemic by design and shows up symmetric.

CompensatorySpinal-originRheumatic

One side narrows the search. Two sides opens it.

HOW I DECIDE

Screen before you treat.

When the pain is bilateral and matches across both sides, I run the rheumatic and neurologic screens before touching tissue. If the screens are clean, we treat the load story. If they flag, the patient sees the right doctor first.

WHAT I TELL THEM

“Both sides matching is data. It tells me to look at the system or the spine before I look at the muscles.”

WHAT CHANGES

Same complaint, three different roads.

A bilateral compensatory pattern still gets PT first. A rheumatic flag means rheumatology and bloodwork before progressive loading. A central spinal flag means an imaging conversation with the medical team. Same complaint, three different roads.
