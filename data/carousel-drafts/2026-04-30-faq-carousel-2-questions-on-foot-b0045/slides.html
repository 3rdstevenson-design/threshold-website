FAQ:TIGHT VS WEAK

Is my main issue tight muscles or weak muscles?

Almost always both. The tightness is the stiffness you feel standing around. The weakness is the strength gap you feel when you load the task. Treating one and skipping the other is how patients stall out.

WHAT I TELL THEM

“It's a combination — things are imbalanced, things are tight. The tightness is the stiffness you feel standing around, and then the strength isn't there for the tasks you want to do.”

3/7
03
WHY BOTH MATTER

Tight AND weak coexist. We address both.

Stretching gives the muscle room to fire. Strength teaches it to fire when the task asks. If you only stretch, the task still fails. If you only strengthen, the tight tissue limits how much of that strength you can use.

Stretch for roomStrength for outputPair them together

The body rarely gives you a clean either/or. Treat the picture, not the label.

FAQ:RUNNING & JOINTS

Is running bad for my joints?

In a vacuum, theoretically yes. Outside the vacuum, your form, your volume progression, your hip mobility, and which muscles do the work all rewrite that answer. Running damages joints when the muscles stop doing their job.

MY ANSWER, IN FULL

“In a vacuum — if you don't look at any other factors — theoretically yes. But nobody runs in a vacuum. Context matters: your running form, your volume progression, whether your hips are stretched and the glutes fire, whether your quads are monopolizing the movement. The joint gets damaged when the muscles aren't doing their job.”

5/7
05
WHY CONTEXT WINS

Nobody runs in a vacuum.

The variables that decide whether running is safe live outside the joint: hip range, glute timing, quad dominance, weekly mileage, surface, and how fast you ramp. The joint gets damaged when those upstream pieces fall out of order.

FormVolume progressionHip mobilityGlute timingQuad dominance

The joint is the messenger. The muscles upstream are the cause.
