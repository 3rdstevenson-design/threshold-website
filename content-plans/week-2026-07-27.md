## Tuesday — 2026-07-28
### Carousel: "Restoration. Accumulation. Intensification. Most programs only run the first one."

- **Pillar:** 2 — Movement Science & Framework Education
- **Type:** Action
- **Entry:** #27 — all three audiences × the system stops too early × a clinician who finishes the job
- **Slide-by-slide outline:**
  1. Hook slide — the three phase names, and the line that most programs only run the first.
  2. Restoration: what it's for (get the tissue tolerant, get range back, calm the system down). This is the part everyone does.
  3. Accumulation: build real volume back into the movement. Where the tissue actually gets capacity, not just permission.
  4. Intensification: load, speed, and the demand that looks like the thing you're going back to. This is the phase almost nobody reaches.
  5. What it feels like from the inside when you only got phase one: you're better, you're cleared, and you still can't do the thing.
  6. How to tell which phase you're actually in, in one question: can you do the thing you got hurt doing, at the intensity you'll need to do it?
  7. CTA slide — handle + booking link + "It's time to cross your threshold."
- **On-screen text seeds:**
  - "Rehab has 3 phases. Most people only get 1."
  - "Restoration gets you tolerant. It doesn't get you capable."
  - "Accumulation is where capacity gets built."
  - "Intensification looks like the sport. That's the point."
  - "Cleared and capable are two different phases."
- **Length:** 7 slides.
- **Voice notes:** Frame this as the map, not the grievance. Do not open on "rehab stopped too early" (that exact shape is the most-skipped opener in the library). Credit the Restoration → Accumulation → Intensification sequence to the Rehab-U work if the caption has room. No em dashes on slide.

## Thursday — 2026-07-30
### Carousel: "The dip is data. It's the part of recovery nobody drew you a picture of."

- **Pillar:** 3 — Philosophy & Mindset
- **Type:** Analytical
- **Entry:** #1 — active adults 35-55 × incomplete rehab, discharged too early × full return to the sport and the identity
- **Why this one:** direct follow-up to the top evergreen reel ("It would be really nice if recovery looked like a straight line"), which is 30 days old and still pulling 2,478 views/day. The brief flags it for a follow-up by name. That reel named the problem. This one gives the reader something to do with it.
- **Slide-by-slide outline:**
  1. Hook slide — the straight line everyone was promised, drawn next to the line recovery actually makes.
  2. Why the dip happens: you added load, the tissue answered, and the answer takes a few days to show up as adaptation.
  3. The distinction that matters: soreness that settles inside 48 hours is a response. Pain that climbs across sessions is a signal.
  4. What I actually track instead of how it feels today: what you can do this week that you couldn't last week.
  5. The psychological half. A dip only reads as failure if nobody told you it was coming. Once you can name it, you stop quitting in it.
  6. What to do in the dip: hold the plan, adjust the dose, keep the movement. Deleting the movement is what costs you the month.
  7. CTA slide — handle + booking link + "It's time to cross your threshold."
- **On-screen text seeds:**
  - "Recovery is not a straight line. Nobody drew you the real one."
  - "The dip is the tissue answering."
  - "Settles in 48 hours = response. Climbs across sessions = signal."
  - "Measure the week, not the morning."
  - "Change the dose. Don't delete the movement."
- **Length:** 7 slides.
- **Voice notes:** Take a position, don't hedge. No negation-correction ("it's not X, it's Y") anywhere, including on-slide. If Lars wants extra reach, slide 1 and slide 3 cut cleanly into a 30s reel using the same audience the original evergreen reel already found.
