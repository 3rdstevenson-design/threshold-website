## Draft A, Research-grounded Reel
**Hook:** I can tell you by week 3 which teams are going to tear hamstrings, because that's when the prevention program quietly stops.

**Spoken script (word for word):**
Okay, so a new meta-analysis pooled 15 trials and 7,465 football players, and it looked at every hamstring prevention program in the literature, Nordics, sprint exposure, all of it, and the one variable that predicted who got hurt was adherence.

Whether they kept doing it.

Teams at 75% adherence or better cut hamstring injury risk by roughly 64%. Teams under 75% got nothing. Their risk ratio was 0.92, which is basically the same as running no program at all.

And every 10% bump in adherence dropped risk another 17%, so it's kind of a dose response on showing up.

Here's what I do with that. I bury the hamstring work inside the warmup where it can't get skipped, I keep it to 2 movements the athletes can run without a coach standing over them, and I track completion the same way I track weight on the bar.

The certainty rating on this evidence is low, so hold the exact numbers loosely. The principle holds. A program you finish beats a better program you abandon.

## Draft B, Hot take Reel
**Hook:** A lineman asked me which supplement would fix his stalled bench, and the honest answer was his conditioning.

**Spoken script (word for word):**
Okay, hot take. The shortcut everybody is shopping for is the thing they already know how to do.

Episode 400 of the Flex Diet Podcast had Dr. Dwayne Jackson on, he's a PhD in neurovascular and exercise physiology, and his line was that there has never actually been an exercise mimetic. Not one.

The peptide crowd is selling MOTS-c and SS-31 as a way to get the mitochondrial adaptations of hard aerobic work without doing the hard aerobic work. Those compounds sit downstream of training. If you aren't pushing the mitochondria in the first place, there's nothing there to amplify.

Now here's the part that matters for a 17 year old lineman. Conditioning is the rate limiter on strength. When you feel like you're going to throw up between every set, you cut the volume, you cut the intensity, and you cap the entire training block. The ceiling is aerobic.

So build the base. Sled pushes and bike intervals and position specific work, 2 days a week through the offseason, and keep a version of it in season.

I'll hedge the evidence, because the mimetic literature is almost entirely preclinical and the human performance data is thin. I won't hedge the recommendation. Do the work.
