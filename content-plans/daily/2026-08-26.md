A kid came back from ACL surgery with a knee that tested clean and an ankle that couldn't hold him.

Okay, so there's a study out this month comparing 2 graft choices for ACL reconstruction. The hamstring tendon, which most people know about, and the peroneus longus, which is a tendon running down the outside of your lower leg into your foot. 12 months out, both groups scored the same on the questionnaires. Knee scores fine. Everybody looked recovered on paper.

Then they actually tested them. The peroneus longus group had hop test asymmetry between 14 and 21 percent. The hamstring group was under 8. And the deficits showed up in ankle plantar flexion and ankle eversion, which is exactly where they took the graft from, and those athletes swayed more and took longer to stabilize when they landed.

So here's what I take from it. Surgery leaves a second problem behind, at the site they borrowed from, and it rarely gets a rehab plan of its own. If you had a peroneus longus graft, your calf and the outside of that ankle need real loading, and your hop symmetry number is telling you about more than your knee.

Ask which graft you got. It changes what you train.

## Draft B — Hot take Reel
**Hook:** Your combine numbers barely predict whether you actually get drafted.

**Spoken script (word for word):**
Your combine numbers barely predict whether you actually get drafted.

New study out this week, 3,681 NFL combine participants, every athlete who completed all 6 tests. The researchers broke the testing down into what it's really measuring, momentum and power and change of direction and strength, and then they asked one question. Does any of it predict who gets picked?

Barely. The prediction models came back poor. And in a few position groups, the undrafted guys actually tested better than the drafted guys.

Now I want to be careful here, because the testing does do something. It explained a real chunk of the physical difference between players at the same position, so it tells you where an athlete is thin. The authors say it plainly. Once you clear the underlying physical thresholds, the numbers stop separating you.

That's the part that matters for a high school kid on the showcase circuit. Clear the threshold. Get strong, get fast, be able to change direction when you're tired. Then go put it on film in an actual game, because that's what's deciding it.

Chasing another tenth of a second past the threshold buys you very little.
