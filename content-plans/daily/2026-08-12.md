## Draft B — Hot take Reel
**Hook:** High bar or low bar, wide stance or narrow. New EMG data says your athlete should stop caring.

**Spoken script (word for word):**
Okay, hot take. The high bar versus low bar argument is eating time your athlete could spend getting stronger.

New study out this week wired up 18 experienced lifters, 9 of them competitive powerlifters, with 14 EMG sensors, and had them squat every way you can squat. High bar and low bar. Wide stance and narrow stance. Heavy triples at 80% and sets of 10 at 50%.

Almost nothing separated the variations. Wide stance drove about 27% more vastus medialis than narrow. Heavy weight drove more vastus lateralis and more semitendinosus, which, yeah, heavy is heavy. Rectus femoris, gastroc, and glute max showed zero difference across every single combination.

The authors' own line is the one I'd put on the wall. Technique and repetition ranges should be selected for comfort of the athlete, as opposed to targeting specific results, because all of it activates the muscles in the legs.

Small study, 18 people, so hold it loosely. But for a high school football or lacrosse athlete, this is easy. Pick the bar position his shoulders tolerate. Pick the stance his hips like. Then go add weight to it for 12 weeks.

The squat is already hitting the whole leg. The argument is worth a couple percent. The bar going up is worth a lot more than that.

Does that make sense?
