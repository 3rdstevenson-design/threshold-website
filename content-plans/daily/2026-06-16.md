## Draft A — Research-grounded Reel
**Hook:** If you want to get faster off the line, your sled needs to be heavier than you think.

**Spoken script (word for word):**
Okay, here's something I see done wrong with speed work all the time. Athletes throw a light sled on, knock out a few sprints, and figure they're building acceleration. They're kind of just sprinting with a parachute that doesn't open at that point.

A new study put adolescent sprinters through 8 weeks of heavy sled work, twice a week, and compared different loads. The light loads barely moved the needle on acceleration. The loads at 40 to 50 percent of body weight, those were the ones that improved the 30 meter sprint with a large effect.

Here's why that matters for a football or lacrosse player. The first 10 to 30 meters is where the game actually lives. That's beating a defender to the edge, closing a gap on defense, winning the ground ball. Acceleration is a strength quality, and to build it you have to give the body something heavy to push against.

So this week, if you're doing sled work for acceleration, load it up. 40 to 50 percent of body weight, short sprints, twice a week. Let it feel heavy. You'll feel the difference in how hard you can punch the ground.

## Draft B — Hot take Reel
**Hook:** Your high school athlete is running on 6 hours of sleep, and the cheapest legal edge for that is sitting in the bodybuilding aisle.

**Spoken script (word for word):**
Okay, hot take. Creatine is the most underrated tool for a high school athlete, and it has almost nothing to do with getting bigger.

Here's the situation. Your kid has school, practice, homework, a phone, and a social life, and they're sleeping maybe 6 hours when they need 9. They show up to practice fried. That under-recovery is the real performance problem most of the time.

A 2026 study loaded active men with 20 grams of creatine a day for a week, split into 4 doses, and they saw better sleep quality, sharper cognitive performance, more physical output, and less muscle soreness. The brain runs on the same energy system creatine supports, so when sleep is short, a full tank helps you think clearly and recover faster.

People still hear creatine and picture a bodybuilder. It's one of the most studied and safest supplements we have, and the upside for a busy, under-recovered athlete is real.

I'm not telling you to skip sleep and supplement your way out of it. Fix the sleep first. But if your athlete is grinding through a brutal stretch of the season, creatine is a smart, cheap insurance policy. Talk to your kid's doctor, then load it up.
