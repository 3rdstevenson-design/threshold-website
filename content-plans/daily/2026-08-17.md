## Draft B — Hot take Reel

**Hook:** The bottom half of the rep is doing almost all of the muscle building work, and it's the half that gets skipped in most high school weight rooms.

**Spoken script (word for word):**

The bottom half of the rep is doing almost all of the muscle building work, and it's the half that gets skipped in most high school weight rooms.

Okay, here's where that comes from. Researchers took 30 trained lifters, gave one arm a full range of motion and gave the other arm only the stretched bottom half, roughly 50% of that person's range, and ran it twice a week for 8 weeks. Muscle thickness came out the same. Strength endurance came out the same. The statistics actually leaned toward no difference at all.

The meta-analysis on this says the same thing. A trivial edge for full range, 0.12, which in a weight room is nothing, and the group training partials at long muscle lengths may have grown a little more.

So the cue I'd change is depth. Police how low the bar gets on every rep, on every set, and treat the loaded stretched position as the whole point of the lift. The quarter squat at the top of the bar path is the one buying you nothing.

I'll hedge the evidence, because it's honest: trained adults, upper body, 8 weeks, small sample. I won't hedge the coaching. Full range stays the default in my programs because it's easier to coach and it protects range. Just quit letting anyone cut the bottom.

30 trained lifters. One arm trained a full range of motion, the other arm trained only the stretched bottom half, about 50% of the range. 8 weeks later the muscle thickness and the strength endurance came out the same.

The meta-analysis backs it up. A 0.12 edge for full range, which is nothing you'd ever feel, and partials at long muscle lengths may actually have an advantage for size.

That changes what I police in the weight room. Depth, every rep, every set. The stretched position under load is where the adaptation lives, and the quarter squat at the top of the bar path is the part earning nothing.

Fair caveats: trained adults, upper body, 8 weeks, small sample. Full range stays my default because it's easier to coach and it keeps range. The bottom is what I refuse to give up.
