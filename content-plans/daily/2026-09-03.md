## Draft B — Hot take Reel
**Hook:** I watched a team finish a conditioning circuit completely gassed, and almost none of that built the engine they need in the fourth quarter.

**Spoken script (word for word):**
I watched a team finish a conditioning circuit completely gassed, and almost none of that built the engine they need in the fourth quarter.

Okay, so heart rate is a terrible cardio detector. You superset trap bar pulls with med ball throws, you cut the rest to 30 seconds, the watch says 175, and it feels like the hardest conditioning of the week. Feeling wrecked and building an aerobic system are two separate things.

There's a 2025 meta-analysis in Frontiers in Physiology on metabolic resistance training in trained athletes. Sprint performance improved. Jump height improved. VO2 max drifted in the right direction and never reached significance.

So that circuit is doing something real. It's building power and repeat effort, and I want it in the program.

The aerobic engine, the thing that decides whether the fourth quarter looks like the first, wants a lot of muscle working at low force, continuously, for a long time. A circuit lift never gives it that.

So keep the lift a lift. If your athletes' lungs are what ends the set, the weight room is doing the wrong job that day.

Then put real aerobic work somewhere else in the week, and test it, so you actually know whether it's moving.

Your team's "conditioning" circuit is a power session wearing a conditioning costume.

2025 meta-analysis in Frontiers in Physiology, 11 studies, trained athletes. Metabolic resistance training improved sprint performance and countermovement jump height. VO2 max moved a little and never reached statistical significance.

That's a useful result, and it's honest about what the tool does. Short rest and mixed stations build power and the ability to repeat efforts. They're a weak lever on the aerobic system.

An elevated heart rate during lifting comes largely from muscle squeezing down on blood vessels and pressure climbing. Continuous aerobic work is a different problem for the body: large amounts of muscle at low force, demanding oxygen for a long time, with cardiac output staying up the whole way.

So in August, when the schedule is tight and the circuit looks like it kills two birds, it mostly kills one.

Keep the lift a lift. Give the engine its own session. Then test it, a repeatable time trial or a fixed distance, so you know whether it's actually moving.
