## Draft B — Hot take Reel
**Hook:** The knee cave you circle on a phone video is not measuring the thing you think it's measuring.

**Spoken script (word for word):**
Okay, hot take. The 2D knee valgus screen, where somebody films an athlete doing a single-leg squat and circles the moment the knee falls in, is a rougher instrument than the confidence people have in it.

New paper in the International Journal of Sports Physical Therapy, August 1st. They ran adolescent athletes through a three-task screen, single-leg squat, single-leg drop landing, and double-leg vertical jump, with real 3D motion capture going, and then they compared the moment the knee looked most medial against the moment peak knee abduction actually happened.

Those two moments landed in different places. On the double-leg jump landing, the knee hit its most medial position at about 18 percent of the phase, and peak knee abduction showed up at 58 percent. That's most of a landing apart.

And here's the mechanical piece. At the frame where the knee looked its worst, the tibia was rotated more externally, which runs sideways to the classic valgus story I got taught.

So keep filming it, it's cheap and it does tell you how the whole leg organizes itself. Just hold the finding loosely, because it's catching a different thing at a different moment than a knee abduction measurement, and an athlete gets cleared or held out on calls like that.

I still like the screen. I like it as a conversation starter, and stuff.
