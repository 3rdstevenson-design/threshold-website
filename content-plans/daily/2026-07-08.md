## Draft A — Research-grounded Reel
**Hook:** Want your athlete faster and more durable? Start by looking at what time practice actually ends.

**Spoken script (word for word):**
Okay, there's a new study out of a high performance sports school that tracked 26 young athletes for 8 weeks, and it found something every coach and parent should hear. When practice or training ended after 8:30 at night, those kids got significantly less total sleep. And every extra 10 minutes of training in a day chipped away a little more sleep on top of that.

Here's why I care. Sleep is where the adaptation happens. It's when the nervous system resets, tissue repairs, and the work your athlete put in on the field actually turns into speed and strength. Short that window and you're training hard for a smaller return, and you're walking into the next session under recovered, which is exactly when the soft tissue injuries tend to show up.

The researchers were blunt about the fix. Finish training by 8:30 so the athlete can still get a minimum of 8 hours. That's it.

So Monday, here's what I'd do. If practice runs late, protect the back end of the night. Lights out on a schedule, no late film, no late scrolling. And if you control the calendar, pull the session earlier or trim the tail end of a long night. Speed of movement in the weight room, quality reps on the field, none of it sticks if the athlete isn't sleeping.

Does that make sense? The cheapest performance upgrade your athlete has is the one that's free.

## Draft B — Hot take Reel
**Hook:** Here's my hot take: those endless conditioning gassers are a low-return way to build an athlete's engine.

**Spoken script (word for word):**
Okay, hot take. If your plan for building an athlete's engine is hours of slow Zone 2 or endless gassers until someone throws up, you're spending a whole lot of time for a small return.

Here's what the research keeps showing. A 2025 meta-analysis of 23 studies in kids and teens found that low volume, high intensity intervals produced a big jump in VO2 max, and the effect was right in line with long steady state cardio, in a fraction of the time. Short, hard, organized. Same engine, way less clock.

And this matters for a field sport athlete even more than for an endurance guy. Football and lacrosse are stop and go. A hard effort, a rest, a hard effort, a rest, over and over. The aerobic engine is what refills the tank between those efforts, so your athlete is just as fast on the last rep as the first. Dr. Mike T Nelson calls this smart cardio for lifters who don't want to lose their gains, and I think that framing fits athletes too. You build the engine, and you don't torch the legs the day before practice.

So Monday, I'd keep the base easy and short a few days a week, then add one real interval day. Something like 4 rounds of 3 to 4 minutes hard with full recovery. Build the engine, keep the legs, save the field for the sport.

That's my take. Steady state still earns a spot in the week. I'd just spend more of the budget on short, hard work.
