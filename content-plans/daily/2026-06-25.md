## Draft A — Research-grounded Reel
**Hook:** Agility ladder work looks like warmup filler, and the research says it actually makes athletes faster.

**Spoken script (word for word):**
Okay, so most of the speed work I see with football and lacrosse athletes is straight-line sprinting, which is great, but the game is rarely a straight line. It's planting, cutting, reacting, and changing direction under pressure.

There's a randomized trial on young soccer players where one group added speed, agility, and quickness work three times a week for eight weeks, the footwork drills, the lateral shuffles, the ladder stuff, the reactive starts. The group that did the SAQ work significantly improved their long sprint speed and their change of direction compared to the group that trained as usual.

Here's why that matters. Change of direction is its own skill. You're decelerating, you're reorienting, and you're reaccelerating, and your body learns that pattern by drilling that pattern. You have to train the cut to own the cut.

So Monday, I'd add two or three short blocks of multidirectional work to your warmup or your speed day. Lateral shuffles, crossover runs, quick footwork into a sprint, react to a call and go. Keep the reps short and crisp, full recovery, quality over grind.

Do that for eight weeks and you get harder to guard. Does that make sense?

## Draft B — Hot take Reel
**Hook:** Keto makes you an incredible fat-burner. Whether it makes you faster is a different story.

**Spoken script (word for word):**
Okay, hot take. The keto versus carbs war is the wrong fight for an athlete.

There's a 2026 meta-analysis, 33 studies, trained endurance athletes, looking at low-carb and ketogenic diets. And the fat-burning numbers are wild, every single study that measured fat oxidation saw it go up, some by almost 200 percent. So yeah, keto makes you an elite fat-burner.

Here's the catch. Max aerobic capacity, your VO2max, was preserved in about half the studies and improved in roughly 1 in 10. So it mostly didn't help anyone get faster. And your submaximal economy, how efficient you are at race pace, that's where it took the biggest hit.

Now think about football and lacrosse. Your sport is repeated sprints, collisions, and high-intensity bursts, and that work runs on carbs. Starve the system that fuels your best efforts and you just end up tired and flat.

So here's my actual position. Fuel for the demand. Eat carbs around your hard training and your games, lean on fat for the easy aerobic days, and let your metabolism stay flexible enough to use both. Fuel the work you're actually asking your body to do.

Be Good. Help Someone. Learn Lots.

**Caption (copy-paste ready):**
Keto will turn you into an elite fat-burner. Whether it makes you a better athlete is a different question.

A 2026 meta-analysis pooled 33 studies on low-carb and ketogenic diets in trained athletes. Every study that measured fat oxidation saw it climb, some by nearly 200%. Impressive.

The performance side is quieter. VO2max was preserved in about half the studies and improved in roughly 1 in 10. Running economy at submaximal pace, how efficient you feel at game speed, took the biggest hit.

Football and lacrosse are repeated sprints, collisions, and high-intensity bursts. That work is carb-powered. Starve that system and you show up flat for the efforts that decide games.

Fuel for the demand. Carbs around hard training and games, fat for the easy aerobic days, a metabolism flexible enough to use both. Fuel the work you're actually asking your body to do.
