## Draft B — Hot take Reel
**Hook:** Your watch says you're 90% recovered. It has no idea you're under-fueled and running on empty.

**Spoken script (word for word):**
I lied, kind of, when I told athletes to trust their recovery score. Let's talk about why that green number on your wrist can straight up fool you.

Here's the problem. Heart rate variability, the thing your Oura or your Whoop turns into a recovery score, is a real signal, but it's a noisy one, and it does not mean what the app tells you it means.

The one that gets athletes in trouble is fueling. When you're under-eating, your HRV can actually look great, it can read like excellent recovery, while your body is quietly clamping things down to save energy. So the app flashes green, and you're really just digging a deeper hole.

And a single day means almost nothing. Your HRV bounces around, it drops after a hard session, that's normal, that's supposed to happen. Reacting to one green or one red morning is how you end up chasing your own tail.

So here's how I'd actually use it. Watch the trend over a couple of weeks, not the daily score, and build your own baseline before you read anything into it. And when the number and your body disagree, your body wins.

I like wearables. I just won't let a wrist gadget overrule an athlete who's telling me they're cooked.
