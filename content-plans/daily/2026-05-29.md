## Draft B — Hot take Reel
**Hook:** I'm a PT, and I'm telling you the manual therapy versus exercise war is over.

**Spoken script (word for word):**

Okay, here's a fight I've been hearing inside physical therapy for my entire career. Manual therapy versus exercise for chronic low back pain. Which one wins?

A 2025 meta-analysis from the European Journal of Pain just answered it. Six randomized trials. 743 patients with chronic non-specific low back pain. Exercise versus manual therapy as a standalone treatment.

The result? No clinically relevant difference, right? Pain intensity was a tie. Disability favored exercise slightly at six months, but the size of the effect was too small to matter on its own.

So here's my hot take. The hands-on versus exercise turf war is a waste of clinical energy. Pick the lever that gets the patient moving better, and stop selling the religion.

In my clinic, here's what I actually do. If a patient's locked up and afraid to move, I use my hands first to give them a window. Then I load the spine inside that window the same day. Manual therapy buys me the door. Loading's what keeps it open.

If your PT spends 45 minutes of an hour on a table doing soft tissue and 15 minutes on three exercises, the math is upside down. Hands open the door. Loading walks you through it.
