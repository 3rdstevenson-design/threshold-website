## Draft B — Hot take Reel

**Hook:** Your athlete has run 5 programs since January. That's why they're not stronger.

**Spoken script (word for word):**

Okay, hot take. Your athlete has run 5 programs since January, and that is the reason they're not stronger.

There's a systematic review in the Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research that asked this exact question. 8 studies, 241 lifters, comparing people who kept the same exercises against people who rotated them.

The finding is pretty specific. Some systematic variation helps, it fills in the regions one exercise misses. And then the line that matters, and I'll read it: excessive, random variation may compromise muscular gains. The authors go further, they say a high frequency of change may actually hinder muscular adaptations.

I heard the same idea on Mike T Nelson's podcast this morning with Zach Moore, and it was the sharpest thing in the episode. Back when you found a workout in a book, you had no idea what results anybody else got from it, so you trusted it, you ran it, and you worked hard. That's most of the magic, right?

So here's my rule. Pick 4 main lifts, run them 8 to 12 weeks, and let the weight on the bar be the thing that moves. Keep the variety in the accessories, where it costs you nothing.

If your athlete can't tell you what their squat did since June, you already have your answer.
