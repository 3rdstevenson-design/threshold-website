## Draft A — Research-grounded Reel
**Hook:** Your worst mood tomorrow might be a sleep problem, not a life problem.

**Spoken script (word for word):**
Okay, here's something I keep seeing in my athletes and honestly in myself. You have a rough day, you sleep on it, and the next morning it feels smaller. That's not a metaphor. There's a stage of sleep doing that work for you.

It's REM sleep, the dreaming stage. Researchers think one of its jobs is to take the emotional charge off yesterday's experiences, so the memory stays and the sting fades. Your brain replays the day in a calmer chemical state and turns the volume down on it.

In one study they selectively cut people's REM sleep, kept the rest of their sleep mostly intact, and the next day those people reported more negative mood and their threat centers, the amygdala, fired harder during a social-stress task. Same stress, bigger reaction, because the overnight reset didn't happen.

Here's why I care as a PT. If you're rehabbing an injury or pushing hard in training, you're carrying physical stress and emotional stress at the same time. Short sleep doesn't just slow tissue healing, it leaves you more reactive, more anxious, kind of raw. And then we wonder why the rehab feels harder than it should.

So before you add another supplement or another recovery gadget, protect the back half of your night. That's where most of your REM lives. Late alcohol and a 5am alarm both steal it first.

Sleep is a recovery tool. Probably the best one I've got, and it's free.

## Draft B — Hot take Reel
**Hook:** If you ice bath right after lifting, you might be deleting your own gains.

**Spoken script (word for word):**
Let's talk about the ice bath, because it's everywhere right now and I think the timing is wrong for most people.

Here's the deal. When you lift, you create a signal. The muscle basically asks the body to build, and that signal runs for hours after you train. A cold plunge right after a hard lifting session blunts that signal. You're telling your body to calm down at the exact moment you wanted it to build up.

A meta-analysis pooled the studies on this, cold water immersion right after resistance training, and the muscle-growth response came out smaller compared to just training and recovering normally. Interesting part, strength held up better than size in some of that data, so it hits the building of new muscle harder than the strength itself.

I'm not anti cold. Cold plunges feel great, they can help you feel recovered, and if your only goal that day is to bounce back for tomorrow's game, fine. The problem is stacking it right on top of a session where the whole point was to get bigger and stronger.

So here's my call. If you're chasing muscle, put space between the lift and the cold, a few hours at least, or save the plunge for rest days. Don't pay for a hard session and then refund it 10 minutes later.

Train hard. Let the signal run. Then chill.
