## Tuesday — 2026-08-18
### Carousel: "Your shoulder holds up at 2,000 yards and quits at 4,000. That is a capacity ceiling, and capacity is testable."

- **Pillar:** 2 — Movement Science & Framework Education
- **Type:** Action
- **Entry:** #3 — young athletes and their parents × fragmented care with no continuity × understanding the body
- **Hook shape:** Statement hook (64.5% skip, +0.4pp, essentially at the all-reel average, n=63, the deepest sample in the brief). Carousels are read, not scrolled past at the 3s mark, so the shape penalty is small here. The slide-1 line still leads with the finding rather than the setup, per the 0-8s rule.
- **Slide-by-slide outline:**
  1. Hook slide — the yardage line. The shoulder that is fine early and quits late.
  2. What a capacity ceiling actually is: the tissue tolerates the demand until the volume passes what it has been built for, and then form goes first and pain follows.
  3. Why this gets missed: an assessment at rest looks clean, because the shoulder is fine at rest. The problem only shows up under accumulated volume.
  4. What I test instead: overhead range under control, scapular position at end range, rotator cuff strength side to side, and how all three hold up after fatigue.
  5. The number that matters: what the shoulder can do on rep 40, not rep 4.
  6. What to do about it in season: build capacity in short doses that do not compete with the practice load. Two exposures a week, speed of movement controlled (3-1-1), volume low enough that Thursday's practice is unaffected.
  7. CTA slide — handle, booking link, "It's time to cross your threshold."
- **On-screen text seeds:**
  - "Fine at 2,000. Gone at 4,000."
  - "A shoulder at rest tells you nothing about a shoulder at volume."
  - "Test it tired. That's when it fails."
  - "Rep 40 is the number. Rep 4 is noise."
  - "Two short exposures a week. Practice stays untouched."
- **Length:** 7 slides.
- **Voice notes:** "Speed of movement" with Ian King notation, never "tempo." No em dashes on slide. No negation-correction anywhere, including on-slide text. Swimming is the obvious read here, so it works for lacrosse, volleyball, and any overhead athlete without naming a sport on slide 1.

## Thursday — 2026-08-20
### Carousel: "Playing through it is a decision, and you can make it on evidence instead of on a feeling."

- **Pillar:** 3 — Philosophy & Mindset
- **Type:** Analytical
- **Entry:** #9 — young athletes and their parents × the wrong ceiling, told to stop × understanding the body
- **Hook shape:** Statement hook, at-average skip, and it puts the entire promise of the carousel on slide 1. The alternative framings all wanted to open by diagnosing the reader's bad judgment, which is the exact shape sitting at 80% skip in the brief.
- **Slide-by-slide outline:**
  1. Hook slide — the decision line. Play through it or sit it out, made on evidence.
  2. The first question: does it change how you move? A limp, a shortened stride, a stroke that has gone lopsided. Compensation is the signal that matters more than the number on a pain scale.
  3. The second question: what does it do across sessions? Settles inside 48 hours means the tissue answered. Climbs from session to session means the load is winning.
  4. The third question: where is it. Joint line, bone, and anything with swelling gets a different answer than muscle belly soreness.
  5. The honest part about the pain scale: a 3 that changes your mechanics is worse than a 5 that does not.
  6. What to do with a yes: reduce the dose, keep the movement, and put the missing capacity somewhere else in the week.
  7. What to do with a no: sit the session, and use it. Sitting out one practice to keep the season is arithmetic, not weakness.
  8. CTA slide — handle, booking link, "It's time to cross your threshold."
- **On-screen text seeds:**
  - "Play through it, or sit it out. Make it on evidence."
  - "If it changed how you move, it already answered you."
  - "Settles in 48 hours = response. Climbs across sessions = signal."
  - "A 3 that changes your mechanics beats a 5 that doesn't."
  - "Change the dose. Keep the movement."
- **Length:** 8 slides.
- **Voice notes:** Take a position on every slide, no hedging. This is athlete-and-parent facing at the same time, so keep it usable on a Thursday night before a Friday game. No negation-correction. Slides 2 and 3 cut cleanly into a 30s reel later in the month if this one performs.
