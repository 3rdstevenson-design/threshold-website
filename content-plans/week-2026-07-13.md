## Tuesday — 2026-07-14
### Carousel: "How to load a cranky tendon without flaring it."
- **Pillar:** exercise
- **Type:** Action
- **Entry:** athletes + active adults × a nagging tendon (patellar, Achilles, elbow) that hurts to train on × a clear way to keep training and build the tissue back
- **Slide-by-slide outline:**
  1. Hook slide — the problem framed plainly.
  2. Why "rest it" fails a tendon (it de-loads the exact quality you need back).
  3. Step 1 — heavy isometrics to calm it down and load it at the same time.
  4. Step 2 — heavy slow resistance as it settles (slower speed of movement, control the whole rep).
  5. Step 3 — add speed and spring back last, once it tolerates load.
  6. The honest caveat: this is a ramp, not a rescue. Progress by how it responds, not by the calendar.
  7. CTA slide — "It's time to cross your threshold." / handle + booking link.
- **On-screen text seeds:**
  - "Your tendon doesn't need rest. It needs the right load."
  - "Isometrics first. They calm it and load it at once."
  - "Heavy and slow next. Control the whole rep."
  - "Speed and spring back last."
  - "Progress by response, not by the calendar."
- **Length:** 6–7 slides.
- **Voice notes:** "Speed of movement," never "tempo." Use Ian King notation if a slide shows a prescription (e.g. 3-1-1). No em dashes on-slide.

## Wednesday — 2026-07-15
### Reel: "Rest is not a rehab plan."
- **Pillar:** philosophy
- **Type:** Contradicting
- **Entry:** athletes + patients × the reflex to stop everything when something hurts × understanding that smart load is what actually rebuilds the tissue
- **Script direction:** Take on the advice everyone's been given: shut it down, wait for it to feel better, then go back to what hurt you. State the positive claim directly (tissue adapts to load, and healing without load leaves you weak where you got hurt). Give the practical version: we change the dose, we don't delete the movement. Keep it warm, not preachy.
- **Visual:** Talking head, single take. Maybe one cutaway to a regressed-then-progressed movement.
- **Length:** ~40–50s
- **Voice notes:** Avoid the banned "This isn't X, this is Y" construction. Just state that load rebuilds. No exclamation points.

## Thursday — 2026-07-16
### Carousel: "Why your athlete isn't getting faster (it's not the weight room)."
- **Pillar:** exercise
- **Type:** Analytical
- **Entry:** youth / HS + collegiate athletes × training hard but speed has plateaued × where speed actually comes from, and how to test it without fancy gear
- **Slide-by-slide outline:**
  1. Hook — the plateau most strong athletes hit.
  2. Strength is the base, but strength alone stops paying off past a point.
  3. Speed is force in a hurry, applied into the ground — and that's a separate skill.
  4. The piece most programs skip: deceleration. You can't apply what you can't absorb.
  5. How I check it without force plates: strength symmetry side to side, hop and jump testing, change of direction under fatigue, movement quality.
  6. What to actually train: sprint exposure, plyometric progression, decel drills.
  7. CTA — "It's time to cross your threshold." / handle + booking link.
- **On-screen text seeds:**
  - "Strong but not fast? Strength isn't the ceiling you think."
  - "Speed is force applied into the ground, in a hurry."
  - "You can't apply what you can't absorb. Train decel."
  - "No force plates needed: symmetry, hop tests, COD under fatigue."
  - "Sprint. Bound. Decelerate. Repeat."
- **Length:** 6–7 slides.
- **Voice notes:** No force plates cited or depicted (per brand). "Athletes / active adults," no ages or demographic targeting.
