### Reel: "She made varsity on Friday and by the next Thursday she couldn't finish a practice."
- **Pillar:** clinic_case
- **Entry:** HS athlete (and the parent watching) × "something started hurting and we don't know if it's serious" × certainty about what to do this week
- **Hook shape:** Story — cited from "Story hook: 60.9% (-3.2pp vs average, n=4)," the only shape beating baseline. Built on the "Patient walked in frustrated after a surgeon visit" pattern (39.1% skip): a specific person, a specific week, and the turn already inside the first sentence. The payoff is the collision between "made varsity" and "couldn't finish," so there is no wind-up to sit through.
- **Script direction:** Tell it straight: she made the team, then practice volume roughly tripled inside 7 days while nothing about her summer prep changed. Land on the read — her body didn't break down, her schedule went up 3x and the tissue that complains first is the one that got the biggest jump. Give the fix in the last 10 seconds: cut the optional extra work for 10 days, sleep, and load the sore area on purpose instead of resting it flat.
- **Visual:** Talking to camera, plain wall, then a 4-second cut to loading a single-leg movement under control. No gym-window backgrounds.
- **Length:** ~50s
- **Shoot notes:** "She" is the first word out of your mouth, already at conversational energy — the 0-8s drop (median 27.5pp) is a cold-open-mid-sentence problem, not a script problem. Frame tight, eyes on the upper third, no ceiling above your head. The brief's three drop-off frames all show a flat expression against a busy or empty background, so shoot this against a clean wall with a real face on it.

- **Entry:** HS/college field-sport athlete × "I walked it off and it never quite came back" × getting the actual step back instead of managing around it
- **Hook shape:** Story — same shape as Monday, and slide 1 is doing the same job an opener does. The idea underneath this is close to the flagged reel "Nagging injuries stick around because rehab stopped too early" (80% skip). The *idea* was fine; the *construction* was an abstract thesis with no person in it. Rebuilt here as a scene with a cost attached, which is what the least-skipped openers all do.
- **Slide-by-slide:**
  1. The hook, full-bleed, no subtitle.
  2. What 4 days of rest actually accomplishes: swelling drops and walking stops hurting. That's the whole list.
  3. What it doesn't touch: the ankle's ability to take load fast and off-balance, which is the only version that shows up in a game.

  5. The gap most ankles are still carrying in November, described plainly.
  6. What to do about it in 15 minutes, 3 times a week.
  7. CTA into a conversation, not a download.
- **On-screen text seeds:** "4 days of rest" / "swelling down, walking fine" / "load, speed, and balance untouched" / "eyes busy" / "side to side, no hesitation" / "same calf strength both sides" / "15 minutes, 3x a week"

- **Slide-by-slide:**

  2. Week 3 of a season does this to nearly everybody, and that's real, not imagined.
  3. Question 1: does it hurt in both legs roughly the same? Symmetry is reassuring.

  6. What to do with 3 reassuring answers, and what to do with one that isn't.
  7. When to stop guessing and get it looked at.
