If a return date is the wrong way to decide you're ready, the obvious question is: what's the right way? The answer is testing. Real, objective, repeatable testing that measures whether your body can meet the demands of your sport.

This is the part that separates a clinic baseline from an actual return to performance. Anyone can ask if it hurts. I want to know what you can produce, how symmetrical it is, and whether you trust it under load.

## Strength, measured side to side

The first gate is strength, and it has to be measured, not eyeballed. I compare the involved side against the other one and look for symmetry, because a leg that's 30 percent weaker will find a way to hide that in everyday movement and then fail when you ask it to sprint.

Daily life lets you compensate. Sport doesn't. So I test until the numbers are honest.

## Power and the ability to absorb force

Strength is the floor. Sport is built on power, which is force applied fast, and on your ability to absorb force when you land and decelerate.

Hop and jump testing tells me how you produce and accept load on one leg, and how that compares left to right. How far you can hop on each leg, how softly you land, how much you quietly favor the good side without realizing it. These tests catch the deficits a casual look misses.

## Change of direction and sport speed

Once you can produce and absorb force, the testing has to look like your sport. Cutting, reacting, repeating it under fatigue. A clean change of direction when you're fresh means little if it falls apart in the third quarter.

So the late stage gets messy on purpose. I want to see you cut off the surgical leg when you're tired, because that's when sport happens and that's when people get hurt.

## Psychological readiness

The last gate is the one most programs ignore entirely. If you don't trust the body, you play tentative, and a tentative cut is how the other knee goes. Readiness to trust your body is something I test for and train, the same as strength.

All of these gates are how I decide you've crossed, instead of guessing from a date. That's the backbone of [crossing the threshold from rehab to performance](/blog/crossing-the-threshold-rehab-to-performance), and it's why I lean on [criteria rather than the calendar](/blog/calendar-vs-criteria).
