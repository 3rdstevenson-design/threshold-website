Ask most people when they can return to their sport and they'll give you a number. 4 weeks. 6 weeks. 12. The number feels reassuring because it's concrete. The problem is that the calendar has no idea what your body can actually do.

Time matters for healing. Tissue needs it, and you can't rush biology. Time just isn't a measure of readiness, and treating it like one is how athletes get sent back unprepared.

<CalendarVsCriteria />

## What a date can't tell you

A return date assumes everyone heals on the same schedule and that healing equals capacity. Neither is true. Two athletes at the same number of weeks can be in completely different places, one ready to load hard and one nowhere close.

When the date decides, the athlete who's behind gets pushed forward anyway, and the athlete who's ahead gets held back for no reason. Both lose.

## What criteria measure instead

Criterion-based progression asks a better question: what can you do right now? I look at objective strength compared side to side, hop and jump testing, change of direction, movement quality under fatigue, and honest psychological readiness to trust the body again.

Each of those is a gate. You move to the next stage when you clear the one in front of you, not when a square on the calendar lights up. Time is still in the picture, it just sits alongside the criteria instead of overriding them.

The research has been moving this way for years. A large share of return-to-sport studies still use time as the only criterion, and the field has been steadily shifting toward criterion-based decisions because they lower reinjury risk and restore performance better. The work on the return-to-sport continuum makes the same point: return is a graded process toward sport and performance, not a single date you get cleared on.

## How this changes your recovery

When criteria drive the plan, the whole thing gets honest. You can see exactly where you are, you know what you're working toward, and you don't get launched back into your sport because a protocol said it was time.

That's the difference between guessing and knowing, and it's a core piece of [crossing the threshold from rehab to performance](/blog/crossing-the-threshold-rehab-to-performance). It's also why a clean discharge date can be so misleading, the same way [being cleared isn't the same as being ready](/blog/the-discharge-cliff).
