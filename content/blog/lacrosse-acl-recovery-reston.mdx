If your son or daughter is coming back from an ACL reconstruction and lacrosse is the goal, you're standing in one of the most important developmental windows of their athletic life. How this recovery is handled shapes whether they get back, whether they come back as the same athlete, and whether they spend the next decade trusting that knee or guarding it.

Most parents I meet in the Reston and Herndon area aren't chasing a shortcut. They're worried about the opposite. They don't know who to trust with their kid's body, and they can't afford to get this one wrong. That instinct is correct. An ACL return done well is the difference between a full career and a string of compensations.

## The surgery is the start, not the finish

A good surgeon gives you a stable knee. What happens over the next 9 to 12 months decides whether that stable knee becomes a confident, competitive one. Families underestimate this part, because the early rehab feels like the hard part. The hard part is actually the return-to-sport phase, and it's the part that gets rushed most often.

Here's the uncomfortable truth from the research and from my own practice. Athletes who return to cutting sports too early, or without meeting real strength and movement standards, reinjure at high rates. The second injury is usually worse than the first. Time since surgery is the weakest way to decide readiness, and it's still the most common one.

## What a real return to sport requires

When I bring a young lacrosse player back from an ACL, the assessment is objective and ongoing. I'm not guessing. I test strength side to side and keep testing it. I watch how they land, how they decelerate, how they cut off the surgical leg under fatigue, not just when they're fresh.

The work is built as a sequence the athlete understands. Restore strength and control, then build the ability to absorb and produce force, then layer in change of direction, then sport speed and the chaos of an actual game. Lacrosse is reactive cutting at speed with a stick in your hands and bodies around you, so the late stage of rehab has to look like that, not like a clean gym.

I also do the part most programs skip. I rebuild the athlete's trust in the knee. A young athlete who doesn't trust the leg plays tentative, and a tentative cut is how the other knee or the same one gets hurt next. Confidence gets trained, the same way strength does.

## I've taken an athlete the whole way

This is not theory for me. I worked with a Division I lacrosse player who tore his ACL his senior season. I rebuilt him on exactly this kind of criterion-based plan and had him back on the field in 8 months, fully healthy, for his fifth-year super-senior season. He plays professionally now, and he hasn't been re-injured since.
