The shoulder's been bothering you for a while. Maybe it started after a surgery, a rotator cuff repair or a replacement, and it never fully came back. Maybe it's just been treated on and off for years without ever getting fixed. Either way, pickleball is your crew and your routine, and losing it is the part that actually keeps you up.

Here's what I want you to hear first. The sequence your recovery followed is the ceiling you're feeling right now, and a sequence can be rebuilt at any age. Your shoulder has a lot more in it than you've been told.

## Age explains less than you've been told

There's a habit in healthcare of explaining away a stubborn problem with a number on a chart. The shoulder's worn, you're older now, this is just what it looks like at your age. I understand why that gets said, and in my experience it's usually wrong as a stopping point.

I've worked with plenty of older athletes who heard the same thing and got back to competing once someone actually addressed what was driving the problem. Tissue does change as we age. Age is a context, though, not a verdict. The real question is whether anyone has built you a plan that respects where you are and still expects you to get all the way back.

## Post-surgical shoulders need a real second phase

Here's where a lot of pickleball shoulders get stuck. The surgery goes fine. The early rehab goes fine. You get your motion back and the pain settles. Then the formal rehab ends, and nobody builds the second phase, the one that takes a calm shoulder and turns it back into a competitive one.

Pickleball asks your shoulder to serve overhead, react fast, and absorb load at awkward angles when you reach for a ball. A shoulder that's comfortable with daily tasks is nowhere near ready for that, and the gap between the two is exactly where the reinjury and the lingering ache live.

When I assess a shoulder like this, I look at the whole chain. How your mid back rotates, how the shoulder blade moves, how much your trunk contributes when you reach and rotate. Athletes who keep tweaking the same shoulder are often missing rotation somewhere else, so the shoulder takes a load it should be sharing.

Here's the simple version of how the shoulder actually works. Think of it as a team, not a single part. Your mid-back rotates to let the arm travel overhead. Your shoulder blade glides and sets a stable base for the arm to work from. The rotator cuff, a small group of muscles, fine-tunes the joint and keeps the ball centered in the socket. When the mid-back is stiff or the shoulder blade doesn't control well, the rotator cuff ends up doing everyone's job, and that's the part that gets cranky and tears. For a plain-language tour of the anatomy, [OrthoInfo from the American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons](https://orthoinfo.aaos.org) is a solid place to start.

## What getting all the way back takes

Treatment starts in session one. I address the actual tissue and the way you load it, then rebuild toward the real demands of your sport on a sequence you understand. Range first, then strength, then the reactive and rotational work pickleball actually requires. Progressive on purpose, so the shoulder is prepared for the game instead of protected from it.

That's [crossing the threshold from rehab to performance](/blog/crossing-the-threshold-rehab-to-performance), and it works the same whether you're recovering a [knee after rehab](/blog/why-your-knee-still-hurts-after-pt) or an [ankle that got cleared too early](/blog/ankle-cleared-but-cant-cut). The part that keeps complaining is usually paying for work that something else stopped doing.

## Worth a closer look if

- You had shoulder surgery and it never fully returned to the court.
- The same shoulder's been treated episodically for years without resolving.
- You've been told your age is the reason, and you don't quite buy it.
- Nobody has watched you serve or reach the way you do in a real game.

You're not too old to get this back. You've most likely just never been given a plan built to take you the whole way.
