You did everything they told you. You went to the appointments, you did the quad sets, you followed the home program for 6 weeks. They discharged you. And your knee still isn't right.

If you're searching at midnight for why your knee still bothers you after PT, here's what I tell every athlete and patient who lands at Threshold after a couple of failed attempts. The problem is almost never the knee itself. It's the sequence the rehab followed, and the fact that it was built for a diagnosis code instead of for an athlete who needs to cut, sprint, and compete.

## Standard rehab wasn't built for what you're trying to do

Most physical therapy runs on a 6 to 8 week protocol designed to get you to a functional baseline. Walking with less pain, climbing stairs, getting back to daily life. For a lot of people that's the right goal.

It isn't yours. You want to get back on the field, be first off the line, trust the knee when you plant and change direction at full speed. Standard rehab stops measuring you long before that point. You get discharged when you can do a bodyweight squat with less pain, and then you're on your own to cross the gap between a printout and a sport.

That gap is where most athletes get stuck. The knee feels fine in the clinic and falls apart on the field.

## The knee is usually the victim, not the cause

When I run a full clinical assessment on a knee that won't settle, I'm rarely looking only at the knee. I'm looking at the hip above it and the ankle below it, at how you absorb force when you land, and at what your body learned to avoid while it was protecting the injury.

A knee that keeps flaring months after rehab is often paying for something nobody assessed. A stiff ankle forces the knee to take a load it was never meant to carry. A hip that doesn't contribute leaves the knee to absorb the difference every time you decelerate. Those patterns don't show up in a 6 week protocol that treats the knee in isolation.

That's the difference between treating the diagnosis and treating the system that broke down behind it. Manage the symptom long enough without resolving what's actually driving it, and the limitation stops feeling temporary. It starts feeling like your new ceiling. Your knee is telling you the bridge was never built.

## What a real return looks like

When I work a knee like this, treatment starts in session one. You don't wait 3 visits to feel a difference. I find where the system failed, address the actual driver, and rebuild the way you load and absorb force under the real demands of your sport.

The work gets progressively harder on purpose. Slower speeds of movement, then loaded strength, then change of direction, then sport speed. You always know why you're doing what you're doing and where you're going next. The goal is you cutting at full speed and trusting it.

This is exactly what I mean by [crossing the threshold from rehab to performance](/blog/crossing-the-threshold-rehab-to-performance). A clean discharge note gets you to baseline. The crossing gets you back to your sport.

## When to take a stalled knee seriously

Take a real second look if:

- You finished PT and the knee still limits your training months later.
- It feels fine day to day but flares the moment you play.
- You've been handed the same few exercises by more than one provider.
- Nobody has watched you actually run, cut, or land.

A knee that hasn't responded to two rounds of rehab isn't telling you you're broken. It's telling you the right question hasn't been asked yet.
