### How I work with you

Every patient goes through the same five parts, in this order. I call it the CROSS method.

**C · Assess every joint.** A full evaluation, joint by joint, to find where things broke down and why. You leave the first visit knowing what's going on.

**R · Treat from the first session.** Hands-on treatment starts that day, along with the exercises that hold the change in place.

**S · Follow a plan with a reason.** Every exercise has a place in the sequence, and you'll always know why you're doing it.

**S · Test it, then trust it.** I retest as you go and stay with you until you're back, in person and between sessions in the app.

### Football, rugby and the weight room

My family moved with the Air Force (Nebraska, then Louisiana) before landing back in Virginia when I was in 8th grade. I started football at 7.

In my senior year of high school, a teammate talked my football friends and me into rugby. That first season the team went into nationals as the 6th seed and won it. The ring still sits on my desk.

I played Division III football at St. Olaf College in Minnesota. I was top 5 on my team in every lift and barely played. That gap between the weight room and the field is a big part of why I train athletes the way I do. Six concussions ended my playing days before my senior season, so I stayed with the program from the sideline and coached in the school's performance program.

### Where I learned to coach

At 20 I started an apprenticeship with a strength coach. It took me to Jamaica, working tracks alongside sprint coaches, and then to a year in his gym, stretching his sprinters and running their control drills every day. I've coached everyone from middle school athletes all the way up to the Olympic stage.

That year showed me what elite standards look like, and that the truth, said clearly and quickly, beats the comfortable version. I tell my patients the truth about their progress.

### Why I opened Threshold

I kept seeing people discharged once the pain went down, before they were back to the sport or the training they came in for. I don't believe "learn to live with it" is true for most of them.

Threshold holds the whole arc in one place: rehab, strength, and the return to what you love, with one person who knows your full history. You never re-explain yourself to a new provider. Every session is with me.

Hey {first name}, If you change one thing from the guide, make it this: stretch at a 2 or 3 out of 10.
It'll feel too easy, and that's the point. Push to a 7 or 8 and the stretch reflex kicks in, so the muscle contracts against you while you're trying to lengthen it. You grit your teeth through 30 seconds and the tissue never lets go.
At a 2 or 3, your nervous system reads the position as safe. Hold it until the feeling drops to a 1, then ease in a little further, back to a 2 or 3, and repeat.
Quick check: if you're gripping the floor, making a face or holding your breath, you've gone past it. Back off until your face is relaxed.
Give it 2 weeks. If the same spot keeps tightening back up anyway, pay attention to that. Recurrence is information. Usually something nearby (a hip, a foot, how you load in training) keeps pulling it back, and that's what I look for.
